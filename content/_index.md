## About Me

Welcome to my website! My name is Xinyuan Tian. I am currently a Ph.D. candidate in the [Department of Biostatistics](https://ysph.yale.edu/research/department-research/biostatistics/) at Yale School of Public Health, advised by [Dr. Yize Zhao](https://ysph.yale.edu/profile/yize-zhao/) and [Dr. Denise Esserman](https://ysph.yale.edu/profile/denise-esserman/).

Prior to this, I received my M.Sc. in Biostatistics from [Yale University](https://www.yale.edu/) in 2022, and my dual B.Sc. degrees in Applied Mathematics and Statistics from the [University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA)](https://www.ucla.edu/) in 2020.

My research develops interpretable artificial intelligence and Bayesian methods for complex biomedical data, with applications in neuroimaging, imaging genetics, and electronic health records. My work combines the flexibility of modern AI models with the inferential discipline of statistical modeling, so that complex data can yield findings that are both interpretable and reliable. My methodological interests include Bayesian inference, deep learning, causal inference and mediation analysis, network analysis, and survival analysis. These methodological and applied contributions have led to first-author [publications](/publications/) in *Biometrics*, the *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society, Series C*, *Biostatistics*, and *Statistics in Medicine*, alongside collaborative publications in *Nature Methods* and *Imaging Neuroscience* and conference papers at ICML and NeurIPS. My research has been recognized with the [John Van Ryzin Award](https://www.enar.org/meetings/StudentPaperAwards/) from [ENAR](https://www.enar.org/) and [ASA Student Paper Awards](https://www.amstat.org/) from the sections on [Bayesian Statistical Science](https://community.amstat.org/sbss/home) and [Statistics in Imaging](https://community.amstat.org/imaging/home). Please visit the [Research](/research/) page for more details.

<p class="job-market">I expect to complete my Ph.D. in May 2027 and am on the <strong>2026–2027 academic job market</strong>, seeking tenure-track assistant professor positions in statistics or biostatistics beginning Fall 2027.</p>
